# Limewash Paint Guide

## What the post covers
What limewash actually is (lime putty + pigment + water — not chalk paint, not textured emulsion; translucent, becomes part of the wall chemically). How the coats work: coat 1 = thin uneven wash establishing base; coat 2 = opposite direction crosshatch building cloud depth; coat 3 = optional, for deliberate variation and darkening corners. Critical rule: always assess fully dry — limewash dries much lighter than it looks wet. Surface prep: bare plaster (ideal), matt emulsion (sand first), new plasterboard (mist coat first), brick (yes), gloss paint (no — will peel). Application: wide natural-bristle brush, single cream dilution, crosshatch strokes wet-over-wet, work in 1m sections, never re-work dry areas. Colours: warm tones (terracotta/ochre, warm cream, clay) more forgiving in UK grey light; cool tones (dusty blue, sage, charcoal) need good natural light. Brands: Bauwerk Calci (€55–70/L, best colour range), Earthborn (€18–25/L, UK-made, best for beginners), Mylands (€40–55/L, good for old lime plaster homes), own-mix (€5–10/L, lime putty + pigment). Where: single feature wall most effective, south/west-facing walls best, fireplace breast, whole room possible. Sealing: traditional limewash not sealed (breathes); seal high-touch areas with lime-compatible breathable sealer only. What kills it: second coat over damp first coat, trying to eliminate variation.
