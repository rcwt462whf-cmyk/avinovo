## How to Light a Living Room
**URL:** /blog/how-to-light-a-living-room | **Category:** Living Room

One ceiling light = flat room. Fix: three layers. Ambient (ceiling + floor lamp bouncing off ceiling, LED strip behind TV). Task (arc floor lamp at shoulder height beside reading seat, not in front). Accent (picture lights, plant uplighters, shelf lamps — purely atmospheric). 2700K is the only correct colour temperature for living rooms. Budget: €70–€170 to transform from a single ceiling light. Evening rule: ceiling at 30%, floor lamp and accents on.

## Kitchen Worktop Materials
**URL:** /blog/kitchen-worktop-materials | **Category:** Kitchen

Six materials rated across scratch/heat/acid/maintenance/cost. Marble: etches from acids (lemon, wine, coffee), needs sealing, develops patina — for people who want a living surface. Quartz: no maintenance, acid-proof, €200–450/m installed, no hot pans. Granite: hardest and most heat-resistant, can take pans directly, speckled look reads as dated in contemporary kitchens, €190–400/m. Solid wood: only material that can be fully restored by sanding, oil every 3–6 months, €60–140/m materials. Concrete: industrial, very high maintenance, €380–880/m. Laminate: massively underrated in 2026, acid-proof, zero maintenance, no heat, €20–55/m.

**Pin angles:** marble after 3 years of real use / the only repairable worktop material / granite is underrated / laminate's reputation is outdated / never put hot pans on quartz / wood worktop — the cleaning mistake that destroys it

## Limewash Paint Guide
**URL:** /blog/limewash-paint-guide | **Category:** DIY

Lime putty + pigment + water — not chalk paint, not textured emulsion. Translucent finish that becomes part of the wall chemically. Three coats: coat 1 = thin uneven wash, coat 2 = crosshatch in opposite direction builds cloud depth, coat 3 = optional for deliberate variation. Always assess fully dry — limewash dries much lighter than it looks wet. Surface prep: bare plaster (ideal), matt emulsion (sand first), new plasterboard (mist coat), gloss paint (no — peels). Application: wide natural-bristle brush, single cream dilution, wet-over-wet crosshatch in 1m sections. Warm tones (terracotta, cream, clay) forgiving in UK grey light; cool tones (dusty blue, sage, charcoal) need good natural light. Brands: Earthborn €18–25/L (best for beginners), Mylands €40–55/L, Bauwerk €55–70/L (best colours). Sealing: leave unsealed normally; lime-compatible breathable sealer only for high-touch areas. A feature wall costs under €60 in materials.

**Pin angles:** dries lighter than it looks wet / crosshatch technique / warm vs cool tones in UK light / brand comparison / €60 feature wall / never re-coat over damp / feature wall vs whole room

## Front Door Colour Guide

## Cabinet & Door Hardware Finishes
**URL:** /blog/hardware-finish-guide | **Category:** Hardware

Five finishes scored on tarnish/fingerprints/versatility/durability/cost. Lacquered brass: fixed shine, lacquer chips and fails unevenly, never use brass polish on it, €12–30/handle. Unlacquered "living" brass: tarnishes/patinas on purpose by design, unique wear pattern per kitchen, effectively unbreakable since no coating to chip, €18–45/handle. Matte black: most versatile, worst fingerprint visibility (biggest reported hardware regret), check PVD vs sprayed powder coat — sprayed chips at edges, €5–35/handle. Brushed nickel: underrated, hides fingerprints/scratches via brushed texture, warmer than chrome, €8–20/handle. Chrome: most durable and cheapest, worst fingerprints, cold/clinical look, €4–15/handle.

## Fireplace Surround Guide
**URL:** /blog/fireplace-surround-guide | **Category:** Living Room

Four surrounds scored on heat/upkeep/drama/era-fit/cost. Marble: historic default, unmatched heat performance, stains from soot if unsealed, €800–4,000. Limestone/travertine: most era-flexible, softer texture than marble, hides marks better, €600–2,200. Plaster/render: lowest maintenance, currently most-requested finish, must be heat-rated near firebox (common safety mistake), €300–900. Exposed brick: cheapest when original brick already exists under paint, near-zero maintenance, commits room to a narrow rustic/industrial aesthetic, €150–500 to strip and repoint. Mantel styling rule: three height tiers (tall anchor / mid object / low horizontal), odd numbers, leave a third of the shelf empty, lean large art rather than hang flush. Three mistakes: surround style vs room architecture mismatch, over-centring furniture on the fireplace, skipping heat-rated adhesive/plaster check.

**Pin angles:** the mantel rule of three heights / plaster is the lowest-maintenance surround / why marble needs annual sealing near the firebox / exposed brick is often already there for free / heat-rated plaster mistake / marble vs limestone vs plaster vs brick / leave a third of the mantel empty

## Fluted Panels Guide
**URL:** /blog/fluted-panels-guide | **Category:** DIY

Ribbed MDF/wood/PVC panels with parallel ribs creating shadow play. Width guide: narrow 30–50mm (fine, small rooms), medium 60–80mm (most versatile), wide 90mm+ (needs height and space). Locations: living room and bedroom headboard wall (ideal), kitchen island and hallway (good), bathroom PVC only. Materials: MDF €15–45/m² (prime before painting), solid wood €50–120/m², PVC €20–55/m². Installation: adhesive + pin nails, spirit level every panel, brush grooves first then roller. Three colour approaches: drenched (same colour everywhere — most editorial), contrast, natural timber. Fail points: more than one wall, mixing with other strong textures, wrong rib width.
