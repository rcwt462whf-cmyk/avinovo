The kitchen drawer that won't close properly. The cabinet stacked with appliances used twice. Most of us have accumulated more kitchen equipment than we'll ever use, and the paradox is that it makes cooking harder, not easier.

When everything has a place and purpose, the kitchen becomes somewhere you want to be.

## The seven

**A good chef's knife.** One knife that fits your hand and holds an edge matters more than a full block. I've cooked on this [8-inch carbon steel knife](#) for three years without wanting anything else.

**A cast iron skillet.** Nearly indestructible, improves with use, and does almost everything. The [Lodge 10-inch](#) is the one I'd buy again.

**A Dutch oven.** Soups, braises, bread, pasta — this is the most versatile vessel in the kitchen. [Le Creuset](#) is the aspirational choice; [Tramontina](#) is nearly as good for a third of the price.

**A sheet pan.** Half-sheet, heavy gauge, rimmed. Roasting, baking, everything.

**A wooden spoon.** Two, actually. One for savory, one for sweet.

**A fine-mesh strainer.** Underrated and constantly useful.

**A digital scale.** For baking especially, weight beats volume every time.

That's it. Everything else is optional.
